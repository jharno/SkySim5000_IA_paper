This section presents the impact of different IA model listed  in Table \ref{table:IAmodels} on the measurements from higher-order weak lensing statistics introduced in Sec. \ref{subsec:beyond-2pt}.  At the same time, we compare results between the mocks sampled with linear bias and those populated with uniform random positions to study the impact of source clustering  (SC) on these statistics.

Fig. \ref{fig:Map3_IA} (left) shows the relative  bias caused by IA and SC for the $M_{\rm ap}^3$ statistics for the three lowest redshift bins (auto-bins only).  We can see that the impact of both IA and SC is the largest at low redshift, where SC alters $M_{\rm ap}^3$ by about 10\%, while it reduces to a few percent at higher redshifts. The NLA and TT affect the statistics at the level of 50\% in the lowest redshift bin, but below 5\% elsewhere. These two models behave similarly and will be difficult to differentiate with this statistics. Note that the cross-bins, not shown here, are heavily affected as well by IA. The $\delta$-NLA model has the most impact here, especially for the lowest smoothing scales, where the $III$ signal is strong in our model and maximally affects small physical scales.

The left of Fig. \ref{fig:I3PCF} presents results from the peak statistics, where we see that the SC term plays a negligible role, consistent with \citet{DESY3_Gatti_source_clust}. The NLA model tends to lower the number of strong negative and positive peaks in favour of those with $\mathcal{S/N}\sim 0$, as found previously in \citet{Tidalator}. The $\delta$-NLA model acts similarly, although the relative signature is shifted towards slightly larger $\mathcal{S/N}\sim 0$ values, due to its increased sensitivity to high-density regions (arising from the $\delta$-weighting), which are highly contaminated by IA. Meanwhile, the TT model is distinct, increasing by 10-20\% the number of positive peaks of all  $\mathcal{S/N}$ values. This will likely be strongly degenerate with cosmology, an investigation that we leave for future work.



Finally, the middle and right-most columns of Fig. \ref{fig:I3PCF} show the impact on the integrated 3pt functions $\zeta_\pm(\vartheta)$. The SC term is important at low redshift, and exceeds the sampling variance by more than $1\sigma$ for $\zeta_+$ in the first bin. Higher redshifts are less affected by this, with relative impact of a few percent only.  The impact of IA at low redshift is stronger than for the other probes investigated in this work, erasing most of the $\zeta_-$ signal. The $\delta$-NLA model has the strongest impact, followed by the NLA then TT.   {\it [Add bins 113 and/or 133, 155, 115, 335, 355].}

In all cases, neglecting IA and SC in the model  produces large biases in all non-Gaussian weak lensing statistics, with the most serious effect seen on measurements including the lower redshift bin. Such effects will inevitably lead to potentially dangerous biases in cosmological inference analyses if unaccounted for, which we will investigate in the future.
